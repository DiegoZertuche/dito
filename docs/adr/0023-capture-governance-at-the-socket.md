# Capture governance: scope, pause, and tenure enforced at the socket, versioned

The capture guard runs **in the connection plane, before persistence**: traffic from out-of-scope chats, paused chats, and pre-tenure timestamps is dropped, never written. Pause and tenure windows are evaluated against message timestamps, and history-sync backfill passes the same guard — the Pausar gap is mechanically permanent. Metadata-only events always flow (scoping UI). Guard config is versioned; a scope/pause change is confirmed to the rep only after the plane acknowledges the new version active (no TTL caching of guard rules); a compensating sweep deletes anything captured under a superseded version (bundle rows + `wa_events`), audit-chained. The self-chat (twin thread) is always in scope and `IsFromMe` events there are **not** dropped — both twin echoes and rep replies carry it (verified), and the ADR-0006 affirmation matcher needs the raw rep replies.

Chosen so out-of-scope or paused content never rests anywhere, and "instant descope" is mechanically true rather than cache-dependent.

**Amendment 2026-08-29 (own sends are captured by the executor, under this same guard).** The claim above that "both twin echoes and rep replies carry it (verified)" is **false, and was found false on the rig** (Dito-Core/dito#63, at the #57 steering session). WhatsApp syncs a rep's outgoing messages to their *other* linked devices; the plane is the device that sent them, so it **never receives an echo of its own send**. Four checks ruled out every other cause: the capture path has no from-me filter and explicitly stores `IsFromMe`; `scope_allowlist` held exactly the identity that chat's inbound rows carry; messages composed on the rig *phone* landed correctly; and the executor recorded `send_executions` and stopped. The consequence was that every message the twin sent on the rep's behalf was invisible to the twin the moment it left — a customer chat holding six messages, all inbound, across two slices, while three `customer_send`s had gone out with outcome `sent`.

So capture now has a **second feed**, alongside live delivery and history sync: the executor **records what it sent** (ADR-0005 job 2 — a recording, not a new responsibility) by calling the guard's own entry point, in the same transaction as the execution evidence and the finalization. **The guard rules are unchanged and are not re-implemented**: the same `inScope` decision runs, so nothing enters twin memory that the guard would not have admitted. `scope_allowlist` (what may be *remembered*, this ADR) and `send_allowlist` (what may be *sent to*, ADR-0017) are different lists and may disagree — the write-back is a capture, so the capture list governs it, and a descoped chat with a tapped draft in flight is sent to and not remembered, exactly as it would be had the rep typed the message on their phone. The seam id is composed as an inbound delivery composes it, so if a rail ever *does* echo, the `(source, external_event_id)` dedupe collapses the two to one row.

**The twin's own posts are deliberately not written back.** `system_send` — the twin thread's drafts, acks, notices and the daily disclosure — records its execution and nothing else. Nothing needs them (the twin thread is structurally outside extraction's input under ADR-0033, and every post is already a durable rep-side row that the Q&A already reads back); writing them would put the twin's own boilerplate into the bundle's vector store to compete with real rep and customer content, and would make anything the twin got wrong in a post return later as its own recollection; and the twin thread is the one chat where twin-authored text is *input grammar*. That is a decision with a test on it, not an omission.

**Amendment 2026-09-09 (the chat directory, the fill, and what a scope-out costs).** Slice 6a (spec [#214](https://github.com/Dito-Core/dito/issues/214) decision 7; built as [#222](https://github.com/Dito-Core/dito/issues/222) and [#223](https://github.com/Dito-Core/dito/issues/223); ruled at the boundary ([#225](https://github.com/Dito-Core/dito/issues/225))) gives the guard a third artifact beside the allowlist and the versioned config. The **chat directory** is one row per twin per conversation the rail has shown the plane — the chat's two identities, its kind read off the address space (`self`, `dm`, `group`, `other`), its display name **sealed under the twin DEK** as a payload is, its last activity, and **one anchor**: the id, direction and instant of its newest message. It is content-free by construction and written for every conversation, in scope or not, because *metadata-only events always flow* is what lets the rep choose. Every chat starts off; at pairing the initial history push is dropped at the socket as before. When the rep scopes a chat in, the plane **fills** it: on-demand history requests to the phone, addressed by the chat's LID (the phone-number form is never answered), anchored on the newest known message and paging backward, one chat at a time in one sitting, until a page crosses the twin's ninety-day line — a product default held as a row per twin — or the phone says there is nothing more. Every page passes the same guard, and history is twin memory only; the Ledger stays link-forward. Two facts the boundary established bind the rest. **The directory read serves the dashboard row ids and opened names and never a JID**, so no page, form or URL in rep-web can hold a customer's number; a scope change names a directory row, and the act writes the allowlist row and the plane's request in one transaction. Since the boundary ([#243](https://github.com/Dito-Core/dito/issues/243)) marking a chat is not the act: both scoping pages are one form of checkboxes and one press — «Guardar», or link day's «Seguir», which saves before it finishes the step — and the server diffs the marks the rep left against the allowlist and performs one such act per changed chat, in the page's order, all evidenced by that press; an unchanged press writes nothing, and a mark reads nothing until it is saved. And **a message that arrives while a chat is out of scope is gone**: the guard drops it at the socket and records nothing, and a later scope-in cannot recover it, because the on-demand request returns only messages older than its anchor and the plane pages backward from the newest message it already holds. The rep is told at the moment the hole is made ([#245](https://github.com/Dito-Core/dito/issues/245)); whether Dito ever deletes what a scope-out leaves in the bundle, and whether it ever fills a group (no LID; a different anchor shape), are on the horizon ([#117](https://github.com/Dito-Core/dito/issues/117)). One more thing the fill delivers: a history page's protocol messages land in the rep's self-chat as opaque deliveries ([ADR-0042](0042-opaque-deliveries-are-seam-rows.md) as amended), recorded and never answered. Since the boundary's rulings (built as [#245](https://github.com/Dito-Core/dito/issues/245)) the row also carries **how its last fill ended** — the instant and a word out of the request's own closed set, stamped by the plane at every end and re-derived from the settled request row at every pass, so an end the plane failed to stamp is stamped a second later — and whether the attempt began over history already on the row, which is the fact a scope-out's hole is told from. The dashboard reads the marker under its role and infers nothing from timing. Three consequences of the second rig session ([#225](https://github.com/Dito-Core/dito/issues/225); built as [#255](https://github.com/Dito-Core/dito/issues/255)) bind here too. **`other` covers WhatsApp's own account**, `0@s.whatsapp.net`, read off the address like the broadcast server's `status` — it is a reserved address and not a person, so it is never offered for scoping, and the one row already kinded `dm` was re-kinded by a forward-only migration rather than left to the next delivery; an allowlist row for such a chat counts in nothing the fill card says, and removing it is an operator's act because a migration writes no scope decision. **A mark survives a search**: each scoping surface has exactly one press — link day's «Seguir», Conexiones' «Guardar» in a sticky foot — and the marks a rep makes between two presses travel as directory row ids on their own device, diffed against the same directory read the press uses, so the fence stands (only a chat the rep saw can move) and a second device's move still wins on every chat this rep did not touch. **And one surface names a customer's number**: the binding card shows the chat being bound by the number the rep's phone shows for it, beside the name the directory holds, because there the number *is* the question — the digits and never a JID, which the served-bytes sweep now refuses; the directory read itself still serves ids and opened names and never an address.
