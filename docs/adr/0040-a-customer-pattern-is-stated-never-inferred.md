# A `CustomerPattern` is stated, never inferred — and it is the customer's own sentence

`CustomerPattern` is the sixth Ledger fact type and the one deferred for a whole slice, for a reason that was never difficulty: **its false positives are aperture breaches rather than noise.** A wrong `QuoteGiven` is a wrong number in a record the rep can correct; a wrong `CustomerPattern` is the rep's employer reading a judgment about the rep, and no correction unreads that. FR-30 defines it narrowly as observable customer regularities — order cadence, stated preferences, constraints — and warns by name against *"rep tactics, judgments, or selling approach"*; ADR-0035 calls an entity-less observation slot *"the natural drain for exactly the rep-level inference ADR-0033 exists to prevent"*. The condition for adding it was a labelled corpus able to tell a good one from a bad one, which [#56](https://github.com/Dito-Core/dito/issues/56) built and #61 extended.

**What ships is the narrow reading: a regularity the CUSTOMER STATED, in the words of one message.** Every other fact type is a claim about a single message; a pattern is a claim about repetition, and that difference is the whole design question. It is answered by refusing the difference: what may be recorded is the customer *saying* the thing is standing — «yo siempre pido a principio de mes», «solo recibimos de lunes a viernes» — which is one message's words like every other fact. What does **not** ship is a regularity the twin worked out. Three separate things make that the right line rather than a timid one. The extraction window is the **day** and a backfilled row is refused at the aperture, so nothing in the pass can observe a repetition in the first place. A cadence assembled by counting orders would **double-count a single want**, because [#83](https://github.com/Dito-Core/dito/issues/83) makes a customer who asks at the outset and again after the price file two `OrderRequested`s. And an observed regularity is by definition inferred rather than read — the exact material ADR-0033 keeps twin-side, arriving under a Ledger fact type. FR-31 buys precision with recall; a stated cadence crosses, an unstated one waits. **Revisit condition:** extraction becoming an execution that can read across days (ADR-0037's shape, sequenced behind the execution runtime). Until then, the absence is a decision and not a gap.

**Two fences, and only the second is prose.** *The authorship fence* (ADR-0038): a `CustomerPattern` claims the customer spoke, so it is read in a counterparty-authored message or refused, checked against `wa_events.from_me` in code. Every rep tactic and rep judgment the precision corpus carries is written in the rep's own message, and all of them die there rather than at a prompt — the rep restating a customer's constraint is not the customer stating it, which costs recall exactly as `OrderRequested`'s fence does. It is also the second lock on ADR-0033: every self-chat row is the rep writing to themselves, so the type most likely to tempt an extractor into reading the rep's own notes as a pattern cannot take one as its source even if the pass ever handed it one. *The prompt* carries what the fence cannot reach: a customer's own message stating a regularity about somebody who is not them — the rep, or the trade — including the mixed message that states both.

**Nothing verbatim crosses, and the first cut of this decision got that wrong.** The payload was `pattern` plus `statedAs`, the customer's whole message read off the seam row, on `OrderTaken`'s evidence argument: #66 measured every wrong crossing arriving at 0.95–1.00, so no gate underneath catches an invented pattern and the rep is the only reader who can — but only if the line shows them the words. Both review axes and Codex round 1 rejected it, and they were right. **One customer message can carry a standing preference *and* a judgment about the rep** — *"usted siempre se tarda, pero nosotros pagamos a 15 días"* — so a whole-message copy files FR-30's excluded class verbatim into `ledger.finalized_facts`, under the one type it is named against, on a row that scores CORRECT because the claim is right and the evidence was never graded. The breach arrives through the field added to prevent it, and no reader downstream can see it. The remedy is deletion, not a smaller quotation: a model-chosen span is the shape ADR-0038 already rejected, and it adds negation-clipping on top.

**So the whole payload is one composed clause, and the rep catches a wrong one the way they catch *"Te pidió …"*.** That is the symmetry ADR-0038 actually draws: evidence is required on `OrderTaken` because its sentence claims **the rep acted**, and a claim about the rep's own act has to be evidenced rather than asserted. `CustomerPattern` claims the **counterparty** spoke, like `OrderRequested`, which carries no evidence field and never has — the line names the act, the content and the provenance (*"su chat, hoy 19:23"*), and the rep corrects it with *"N no"* inside the window. What is bought is a stronger wall statement than any other fact type makes: **nothing from the conversation is copied into a `CustomerPattern` at all.** If the model writes a rep judgment into the clause itself, that is a false positive the corpus grades and the per-type row reports, rather than a laundering path with no reader. The corpus grades the clause on both sides for exactly that reason — `contains` for the token that must survive a paraphrase, and `lacks`, added here, for the day's own forbidden line that must not have been picked up along the way.

**The payload shape is settled once, and it is one required field.** ADR-0034 makes evolution additive-only, so the question a permanent shape has to answer is which direction actually costs. Adding a field later is free — an absent one on a v1 row means *the shape had no slot for it*, which reads true. **Removing one is impossible**, and a field that cannot be removed is exactly what the whole-message evidence turned out to be. So nothing else is stored: no `kind` taxonomy (grading it would measure the model's choice of bucket rather than its reading), no observation count, no first-seen date, and no evidence. Those are all addable at v2 as optional fields if a real need shows. `(CustomerPattern, 1)` is a genuinely fresh stamp under a label nothing was ever filed against, so there is no meaning shift, no burned number and no upcaster.

**The orphan rule is untouched (FR-32, ADR-0035).** A `CustomerPattern` attaches to exactly one entity like every other fact, and this ticket introduces no non-entity slot. *"December is slow for construction"* — ADR-0035's own example, and a real message in the corpus — is a regularity, is stated, and belongs to nobody in the conversation: filing it against the cliente whose chat it appeared in would claim they said something about their own buying when they said something about the trade. So it is **not a `CustomerPattern` at all**, and the corpus labels it silent so that filing one costs precision. Where the twin is merely unsure whether a customer said a thing, the below-threshold route already turns the candidate into a question that expires — the shipped behaviour ADR-0035 names, and it passes ADR-0039's test because the sentence is complete and the rep's answer settles it. **A market observation is a different uncertainty and must not take that route**, which is why nothing here builds one: the missing thing is an *entity*, not a truth value, so a *"si"* would file *"Te dijo que diciembre es flojo para la construcción"* against whichever cliente happened to say it — the wrong record we started with, now with the rep's own hand on it. That is ADR-0039's named failure, and asking would be building the surface it forbids. So it is recorded as nothing, and a filed one is an ordinary false positive the corpus grades: `PD-01` is labelled silent, beside `PD-02`, which is the same December said about the customer and does file a fact.

**And it is measured apart, never folded in.** Precision averaged over a recoverable error and an unrecoverable one reports one reassuring number, so the report carries `CustomerPattern`'s own row with its own n — printed even when nothing crossed on it, because a missing row reads as *fine*. Per #56 AC 6 the measurement gates nothing, and tuning the extractor to it is itself the defect.

Chosen so the type that made the Ledger crossing worth building can ship without becoming the way the wall is crossed: the twin records what a customer said about themselves, in their own words, and works nothing out about anybody.

**Amendment 2026-09-09 (the citation corrected; the revisit condition met).** The sixth paragraph above says that where the twin is unsure whether a customer said a thing, *«the below-threshold route already turns the candidate into a question that expires»*. That route is closed: under [ADR-0043](0043-a-question-is-a-service-never-a-plea.md) a below-threshold candidate is **dropped and counted**, never a question, and the paragraph's argument holds without it — a market observation is still an entity-shaped uncertainty, still filed as nothing, still graded silent. And the revisit condition the second paragraph set — *extraction becoming an execution that can read across days* — was met at slice 5b (extraction looks first, [ADR-0039](0039-the-twin-asks-only-what-the-rep-can-settle.md) as amended) and the decision was re-examined and stands, recorded on the horizon ([#117](https://github.com/Dito-Core/dito/issues/117), 2026-09-05): a pattern the twin worked out is inferred, and inferred is what [ADR-0033](0033-rep-notepad-is-twin-side-only.md) keeps twin-side.
