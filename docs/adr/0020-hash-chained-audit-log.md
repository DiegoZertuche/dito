# Hash-chained audit log; content-free everywhere; evidence dies with the key

All G16 trust mechanisms write to an append-only, HMAC hash-chained audit log (caller identity + action type + opaque refs + outcome per entry), human-readable when trust is questioned; CloudTrail KMS events feed it. **Chain entries are content-free — same rule as application logs.** Where dispute resolution needs content, the entry references a DEK-encrypted evidence blob that dies with the twin key: the chain's integrity survives erasure, its rep-content referents do not. Application logs carry opaque ids only.

Chosen so trust claims never rest on mutable logs, and the audit chain can never become the one rep-content store erasure cannot touch.

**Amendment 2026-09-03 (one chain per side; the owner side's is one per company, under its own key).** Decided on [#157](https://github.com/Dito-Core/dito/issues/157). "An audit log" had been read as one table, and slice 4's ruling on #130 had the owner twin append to it — which needs SELECT, because the appender reads and authenticates the tail before extending it, and the tail is whichever process wrote last. That handed the owner credential the rep side's whole chain: the `twin:<id>` refs the seats view deliberately withholds, and every `customer_send`, refusal and `question_answer` with its timestamp — a per-rep activity record that [ADR-0025](0025-telemetry-content-free-wall-respecting.md) forbids deriving owner-side, live on a one-company deployment and not only at fleet scale. So: **the owner side may read only what it wrote.** The rep side keeps its one chain, unchanged in schema, key and append path. The owner side appends to **its own chain in the `owner` schema, one per company by row-level security** — owned by the state role and appended by the login, the shape #159 built — **under its own HMAC key**, so the owner environment holds nothing that can name, read, write or verify a rep-side store: [ADR-0002](0002-wall-is-a-deployment-and-credential-boundary.md)'s letter with no footnote. Which chain an execution appends to is decided by the app that runs it, never by the runtime ([ADR-0014](0014-runtime-harness-tool-runner.md) as amended). `audit-verify` verifies every chain it finds under the key for its side and exits with one verdict: the chains record disjoint acts and no G16 claim orders an owner act against a rep act, so nothing is lost to the split. The owner chain is the audit chain for [ADR-0009](0009-erasure-is-kms-key-destruction.md) and [ADR-0030](0030-company-wind-down-honors-the-export-promise.md): it survives erasure and company wind-down, and is not owner state those delete. Rejected: row-level security on the shared chain (the owner would still read its own company's rep activity); definer functions over the shared chain with a content-free key attestation (one global lock for every company's owner and rep appenders, and a tail hash that changes on any rep's act is itself a fleet-wide activity signal); accepting the exposure (the grant audit would say green over a rep-activity read). The entries the owner wrote to the shared chain before the cutover stay as history; coverage accepts either chain for `execution`, safe because the only grant that could put a new one there is the one FR-71a turns red on.

**Amendment 2026-09-09 (what link day and the dashboard chain, and what they do not).** From slice 6a ([#223](https://github.com/Dito-Core/dito/issues/223), [#224](https://github.com/Dito-Core/dito/issues/224); boundary ([#225](https://github.com/Dito-Core/dito/issues/225))). A **scope switch writes two entries** — `scope_change` and `execution`, joined by the act's id — because the act and the execution that records it are two facts a verifier reads apart. **Five link-day writes leave no entry, each with its reason recorded beside it**: the email write (an account fact in the clear, the same standing sessions have), the acknowledgment (its own append-only row carries the digest, version, instant and session — a second record would be a second authority), the seat's `linked` flip (written by the holder from a rail row that is itself the record), the link ask (a request row with one outcome), and the chat search (a rep's own search term is state on their device, never a URL and never a row). And a **refusal recorded by a keyless minter** — rep-web refusing a second tap under [ADR-0006](0006-send-lock-topology.md) — is an **owed entry**: rep-web holds no chain key, so the refusal row carries the fact until the lease holder chains it at its next pass. Coverage counts it once it is chained; a rig stopped between the two reads the refusal as a row and not yet as an entry, which is the honest state.

**Amendment 2026-09-26 (the pair, when the act's own write is not a row).** A dashboard act commits its act row, the act's own write, the chain entry and the execution record together — one commit, in that order, which is what lets a refusal leave the row and nothing else. The notebook edit ([ADR-0033](0033-rep-notepad-is-twin-side-only.md) as amended today; [#264](https://github.com/Dito-Core/dito/issues/264)) is the first act whose own write is **not a Postgres row** but a version in the twin's bundle, so it cannot share that commit. The rule holds for the pair and bends only around the write: the act row commits **first**, as the class-2 evidence the version names, so nothing in the bundle ever lacks its evidence; the version is appended **under the act row's `FOR UPDATE` lock, inside the transaction that commits the chain entry and the record**, so the write and its account are one holder's decision; and an act whose account never landed is **settled by the lease holder's pass** from the store it wrote, under the same lock, `performed` under the execution id the version carries or `refused` when there is no version. The pair is therefore written exactly once and never contradicts the bundle, whichever of the two writers gets there. Content-free as ever: the entry carries the act row's id and the outcome, never a character of the notebook.
