# The customer asking and the business agreeing are two different facts

**`OrderTaken` had no rep in it.** Its contract was *"the customer placed an order, or confirmed one"*, so «Ah y súmale unas 20 más» filed a confident order for 20 and the owner reading that timeline saw a commitment nobody made — a wrong yes the rep never gave, which is the error direction ADR-0037 exists to protect against. The type is split in two. **`OrderRequested`** is the customer asking, which is everything the old type recorded. **`OrderTaken`** is the customer asking *and* the business agreeing, which nothing recorded before.

**They are two events, not two states.** A request on Monday is an `OrderRequested` dated Monday; an agreement on Tuesday is an `OrderTaken` dated Tuesday. Neither ever becomes the other, nothing is updated, superseded, linked or reconciled, and the two landing in different extraction batches is expected. That is the only shape an append-only store can hold — ADR-0034 forbids restructuring what is stored — and it is also the more truthful one: a customer who asks for 100 and a rep who agrees to 80 discloses as two lines whose numbers differ, where one collapsed line would hide exactly the discrepancy the correction window exists to expose. **Nothing links a request to a later order**, so *"what customers asked for that we never sold them"* is a read-time question — same entity, comparable goods, no subsequent `OrderTaken` — not a stored relationship. The aggregate shape of unmet demand is what has value; exact pairing is not, and would cost a fact→fact relation the Ledger does not have.

**A fact about someone's act is read in that person's own message.** Every disclosure line names an actor — «Le cotizaste …», «Le confirmaste …», «Te pidió …» — and a fact filed from the wrong side of the conversation makes that sentence false in the one way a rep cannot see. So the aperture checks `wa_events.from_me`, the flag the plane stamped, before shaping any payload: `QuoteGiven`, `DatePromised` and `OrderTaken` are rep-authored or they are refused, `OrderRequested` is counterparty-authored or it is refused, and `ContactEstablished` is fenced to neither because its sentence names no actor. It is a **refusal, not a question** — the aperture never asks a rep about something it could not have filed however they answered, and a «si» on a sentence about the wrong person would file the same false claim. This also settles #70's open remainder, which asked the same question about `DatePromised`, with the same answer: a customer's «ok, entonces el viernes» is the customer accepting a promise, and if the rep made one their own message says so. **`QuoteGiven` is fenced too** — a widening neither ticket asked for, put to Diego and confirmed 2026-08-30: *"a customer shouldn’t be able to create a quote, that is only a rep side action."* It is the same defect in a third type («Le cotizaste …» read in the customer's message quoting a competitor's price), it is one row of the same table rather than new machinery, and leaving it unfenced would mean writing down that a quote's sentence names no actor, which is false. The corpus already believed all of this — all 89 human labels put every quote and every promise on a rep message and every order on the customer's, without ever having been told to, so the fence costs no measured recall.

**An `OrderTaken` carries the rep's own message as its evidence, and the aperture takes it off the seam row.** `agreement` is required on the v3 payload and is the one payload field in any shape the caller does not supply: not a span a model chose, but the whole message the fact cites, read back out of `wa_events`. A model-chosen quotation, even one verified as a substring of that message, is the wrong shape here — «si el proveedor me surte mañana, sí. si no, hasta la otra semana» yields a perfectly genuine «sí», and the line would then read «Dijiste: «sí».» with the condition clipped away at the one point the rep was supposed to see it. There is no span to clip when the field is the message, and it is less machinery besides: no wire field, no substring check. A message too long for a payload text field refuses the crossing rather than being truncated — a truncated quotation is a clipped one — and the `OrderRequested` still stands. A model cannot produce a row on the seam, which is the same defence ADR-0003 gives a class-2 rep act, and it is what makes *"evidence of the business agreeing, present in the transcript and never inferred"* a property of the code rather than a sentence in a prompt. **It is required because #66 measured that nothing downstream can catch a wrong one**: every wrong crossing on the corpus arrived at 0.95–1.00, the middle band held 1 of 88, and the emitter has a vocabulary of five values rather than a scale. Where the error is about what a fact *means* rather than what was said, confidence is highest exactly where it is wrong. So the evidence goes where the rep can see it: the line reads «Le confirmaste el pedido de 20 bultos. Dijiste: «déjame checar».» and is wrong on its face, inside the 24-hour window (ADR-0004), where «Le confirmaste el pedido de 20 bultos» alone would be unfalsifiable at a glance on a busy day.

**The quoted agreement is owner-visible, and that is deliberate.** It is a payload field, so `ledger.finalized_facts` exposes it once the shadow flag lifts — there is no other way for the rep's line to render it, since the line is rendered from the payload and nothing else. It is a rep's own message to a customer rather than a note to their twin, which is the line ADR-0033 draws; it is capped at the same 200 characters every payload text field is, and swept for chat identity by the same wall check every payload string gets (ADR-0004). What an owner reads is the evidence for the order — the words that made it one — which is provenance they should have and could not otherwise get. What the rep gets is the same string, in the line they can correct. **For a reaction-evidenced order the evidence is the gesture and the whole message it points at**, composed by the aperture off the two seam rows — the emoji from the reaction, the words from the message its own pointer names — and the line reads «Confirmaste con 👍: «…».». That gesture rides on `agreementGesture`, an additive optional field (ADR-0034): absent on every message-evidenced row, where an absent field means *not recorded then* and reads true, so `agreement` keeps its meaning — the words the rep agreed to — and `payload_version` stays at 3. It carries no upcaster for the same reason the split did not, re-verified when it was added: `shadow` defaults true and nothing flips it, so every `OrderTaken` row that exists is shadow and no owner has read one. **What that field puts in front of an owner is the CUSTOMER's own words**, for the first time in any payload, and it is deliberate rather than overlooked: ADR-0041's worked example is exactly that line, the words are *what was agreed to* rather than colour around it, and there is no other way for either reader to check the filing. It is not ADR-0040's case — a `CustomerPattern`'s verbatim copy was gratuitous, since its payload is a composed clause and the message added nothing the record needed. Whatever else the request happened to say rides along with it, which is the same trade the spoken case has always made with the rep's own message, under the same cap and the same wall sweep (ADR-0004).

**What counts as agreeing is narrow, and it is narrow because the failure mode aligns with the business.** Either an explicit affirmative answering the request — «va», «sale», «listo», «órale», «sí», «claro», «trato hecho» — or a commitment to supply or deliver the thing asked for, which is agreement without the word yes and is recorded as such deliberately rather than left for a model to infer. **A price is not a yes**: a `QuoteGiven` answering a request is a quote and nothing more. Neither is a question back, a hedge («déjame checar»), a condition, or a refusal. And an exchange where it is unclear files nothing, which is *tracking the truth* rather than being timid — if the rep's agreement was not clear to the customer, no order exists in reality either. **The commercial fields are the terms the business ACCEPTED**, not the terms that were asked for: the request's numbers unless the rep's own message changes them, so a customer asking for 100 and a rep answering «solo tengo 80» files an `OrderRequested` for 100 and an `OrderTaken` for 80 — the two-line case this whole shape exists for.

**An agreement the extractor cannot pair with a request files nothing, and the boundary is the window rather than the calendar** (Codex r3 corrected the first wording of this). The fact model above permits Monday's request and Tuesday's agreement, and an `OrderTaken` has to name *what was asked for* — which a bare «va» does not state. So the rule is: file one only when both the request and the answer are among the messages the pass was given. The request may also be the target ADR-0041's renderer inlined on a reaction line: a gesture at an out-of-window message carries that message's own words onto the line, so the pass was given the request even though it is not a line of its own, and the pairing is available (Dito-Core/dito#99). Usually that is one day (ADR-0016's per-chat daily window); a recovery run whose window reaches back past a settled day may hold both, and then the pairing is available and correct. When it is not, nothing is filed and the earlier `OrderRequested` stands alone. This is the same limit #70 recorded for a day named in an earlier message than the quote it belongs to, and it is left as a limit rather than widened for the same reason: reaching across messages for content is precisely what produced #57's wrong order, and there are no corpus labels yet to measure whether a wider reach helps or hurts (#59). It is also where the split's under-filing lives most visibly — a cross-day agreement reads as unmet demand it is not.

**The signal is partly invisible, and that is recorded rather than designed around.** The plane persists only `Conversation` and `ExtendedTextMessage`, so a 👍 on the customer's request — about as clear an agreement as two people can have, and plausibly the most common way a rep says yes on WhatsApp — never reaches the seam (#72). This design does not pretend otherwise: it biases `OrderTaken` toward **under-filing**, in a way no eval on captured text can detect, because the corpus contains only what was captured. Under-filing is the safe direction here — a request that was agreed to and reads as unmet demand is a lesser wrong than an order the business never took — but the number will look fine either way, and that is the part to remember.

**Amendment 2026-08-31 (the invisibility is repealed — ADR-0041, Dito-Core/dito#72).** Reactions now cross the seam as typed events, and a rep's 👍 on the request is admissible as this fact's `agreement`: the model cites the reaction row, deterministic code reads it back and takes the target off the row — the same defence the paragraph above gives the quoted message. The paragraph above stands as the record of why #72 existed. What survives of it is the bias, not the blindness: under-filing is still the safe direction, and the corpus still cannot measure what was never captured — but the most common yes on WhatsApp is no longer in that category.

**It carries no upcaster because nothing non-shadow existed.** ADR-0034 is explicit that a field whose meaning shifts needs a read-time upcaster and its own ADR; this is that ADR, and the upcaster is unnecessary only because of when the change was made. The live rig Ledger held three facts, all shadow-flagged, **zero non-shadow** — and `shadow` is what ADR-0016 puts inside the finalized views, so a shadow row has never been part of anything an owner read. The migration therefore relabels every standing `OrderTaken` row to `OrderRequested`, which is **meaning-preserving**: those rows assert "the customer asked", which is precisely what the new name spells, and the disclosure line each of them was given — «Te pidió …» — stays true word for word, which is why relabelling beats deleting under a rule that forbids rewriting what a rep was told. `payload_version` is untouched, so `(OrderRequested, 1)` is the pre-#70 commercial shape those rows carry and `(OrderRequested, 2)` is the same shape plus `requestedFor`: the new type inherits its predecessor's version line because it inherits its meaning. `OrderTaken` steps to **3** — the numbers 1 and 2 are burned rather than recycled, so a stray row from a database the migration never reached can never be mistaken for the new shape. The migration asserts the window rather than assuming it: it refuses, loudly, if a single non-shadow `OrderTaken` row exists. **This window closes the moment Test 4 flips the shadow flag**, and it will never be cheaper than it was.

**`requestedFor` survives unchanged**, and the constraint was extended rather than re-cut. #70's date field says *which day*, never requested-versus-agreed: it is exact on an `OrderRequested`, still true on an `OrderTaken`, and never becomes false — which matters, because ADR-0034 cannot express a rename without the upcaster this change is avoiding. The `FACT_TYPES` CHECK gained a value by DROP-and-re-CREATE carrying every value it already granted, and `migration-vocabulary.test.ts` now watches that constraint too: a hand-written re-cut from a stale ancestor silently dropped values twice during slice 3, and the sweep fails on any successive cut that is not a superset of the one before.

Chosen because the gap between the two types is business intelligence that exists nowhere else. Orders already live in whatever system a company uses; **unmet demand evaporates in WhatsApp threads**, and *"customers keep asking for X and we keep not selling it"* is a finding an owner would pay for and currently cannot get. Splitting the type is what makes it recordable at all — and it makes the set coherent besides: one type for what the customer wanted, four for what the business committed to.

**Amendment 2026-09-06 (looking is not pairing; the fence is structural; cross-day pairing is owed).** Extraction can search the rep's history now ([ADR-0039](0039-the-twin-asks-only-what-the-rep-can-settle.md) as amended, [#196](https://github.com/Dito-Core/dito/issues/196)), which re-asks the window question above from the other side: a model that can find Monday's request could pair it with Tuesday's bare «va». It may not. **Looking resolves what a request names** — the goods behind «lo mismo del pedido pasado» — and contributes only those values; it never supplies the request half of an `OrderTaken`. The rule «both the request and the answer are among the messages the pass was given» stands word for word, and it stopped being prose: an `OrderTaken` evidenced by a rep message needs a counterparty beat inside the window it was read in, or the pass refuses it — a gesture keeps the exception [ADR-0041](0041-reactions-are-typed-seam-events.md) states — and the seam test submits the forbidden cross-day candidate and goes red without the fence (Codex round 3 on #196). Two things ride with that. The agreement quote is still the whole seam row, so a customer's name the rep typed into another customer's chat crosses inside it; ruled 2026-09-06 to stand — the wall is about chat identity, the quote may not be clipped, and the rep reads the line inside the window. And the under-filing the paragraph above called «most visible» is now a **requirement rather than a limit**. Diego, at the slice-5b grilling ([#179](https://github.com/Dito-Core/dito/issues/179)): *«in the future this won't be acceptable performance — an order taken given the next day should show».* It is on the horizon ([#117](https://github.com/Dito-Core/dito/issues/117)) under *Extraction quality*, with this slice's looking cases as the corpus it needs first, and it will land as a change to this rule with its own measurement — never as a widening of the search.
